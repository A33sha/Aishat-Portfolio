How I think about this work

Product design background

My approach to instructional design begins with structure, not content. Before writing a single learning objective, I map how a program will run at scale: who facilitates it, how it is assessed, and what happens when a step in that process fails.

A background in product design gives this work a technical edge. I have gathered requirements, written user stories, and run user acceptance testing on real products, and I bring that same discipline to instructional design — mapping the learner's journey the way a product team maps a user's, then testing where it breaks before a learner ever reaches that point. It allows me to evaluate a learning experience from both sides: as the learner moving through it, and as the instructional designer accountable for how well it holds up.

Much of instructional design work is invisible in the final product. It is the standard operating procedure that keeps delivery consistent across facilitators. It is the rubric that keeps an assessment fair across thousands of learners. It is the template that lets the next designer build on a foundation instead of a blank page.

The work

Designing at national scale

Building the instructional design behind digital skilling programs that need to hold together for thousands of learners at once, not just one classroom.

  • Designed learning experiences across four major programs, collectively reaching over 28,000 participants.
  • Ran a full fellowship program end-to-end: recruited, onboarded, and managed over 40 SMEs across 15+ technical pathways, and coordinated 70 capstone teams aligned to the UN SDGs.
